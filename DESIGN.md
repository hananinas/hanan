# Design direction

This is a personal work notebook, not a software launch page. Someone should be able to learn who Hanan is and open a project in a few seconds. The aesthetic is a quiet index: a generous introduction, compact work rows, and a single readable column for articles.

## Decisions

- One warm-neutral paper background, dark green-gray type, quiet green links. No dark-mode duplicate, gradient hero, shadows or floating widgets.
- Manrope for headings and body, DM Mono for dates and small metadata only. Headlines do the work. Body prose stays around 68 characters per line with generous line height.
- Align each project row by date and title. On narrow screens, the date moves above the title. Use plain rules to separate items, not cards.
- Keep the real diagrams and screenshots inside historical articles. Do not use are.na scans or arbitrary stock images as if they were Hanan's own work.
- Navigation is short and visible on mobile. Older `/skils/` links remain live as a topic index, but the main navigation is Work / Studies / About / Search.
- Contact is text, not a call-to-action box. Article pages include a stable back link, publication date, summary and restrained topic metadata.

## Research basis

Ellen Lupton's *Thinking with Type* treats type and its arrangement as structure. Josef Müller-Brockmann's *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* is a prompt to keep alignments consistent, not to force equal cards. Matthew Butterick's open *Practical Typography* recommends readable measure and line spacing. These are site decisions inspired by those sources, not verbatim rules attributed to books we have not read cover to cover. Exact sources and sampled are.na channels are in `research/design-references.md`.

## Reject at review

Gradient or quote hero, icons on every row, rounded colored tag pills, generic self-praise, fake achievements, unreadable long lines, clipped mobile navigation, hover jumps, keyboard-inaccessible links, and a public claim that cannot be checked.
